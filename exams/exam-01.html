SECTION A: MULTIPLE-CHOICE QUESTIONS
1. What is a business organisation?
A. A group of people who meet for entertainment only.
B. An entity established to conduct economic activities and achieve specific objectives.
C. A government department responsible only for collecting taxes.
D. An institution that operates without resources.
2. Which of the following is a primary objective of most private-sector business organisations?
A. To eliminate all competition.
B. To provide services without incurring costs.
C. To generate profit and create value.
D. To prevent consumers from purchasing goods.
3. Which form of business is owned and controlled by one individual?
A. Partnership.
B. Public limited company.
C. Cooperative society.
D. Sole proprietorship.
4. One major advantage of a sole proprietorship is that:
A. It is generally easy to establish and manage.
B. It always has access to unlimited capital.
ZEGU
C. Its owner has no personal financial liability.
D. It must have a board of directors.
5. What is the main disadvantage of a sole proprietorship concerning business debts?
A. The owner cannot make decisions.
B. The business cannot employ workers.
C. The owner may have unlimited personal liability.
D. The business must issue shares to the public.
6. A partnership is generally formed when:
A. A government owns all business assets.
B. Two or more persons agree to conduct business together.
C. One individual operates a business alone.
D. A company sells shares exclusively to the public.
7. Which document commonly sets out the rights, responsibilities and profit-sharing arrangements of partners?
A. Delivery note.
B. Purchase order.
C. Bank statement.
D. Partnership agreement.
8. In a traditional general partnership, partners may be personally liable for business debts. This means that:
A. Their personal assets may be at risk if the business cannot pay its debts.
B. They can never lose money invested in the business.
C. Only the government is responsible for the debts.
D. All business debts are automatically cancelled when a partner leaves.
9. What is a company?
A. A temporary agreement between customers.
B. An organisation that cannot own property.
C. A legal entity established under applicable company law.
D. A business that must be operated by one person.
10. What does separate legal personality mean?
A. A company cannot enter into contracts.
B. A company is legally distinct from its owners.
C. Shareholders personally own every company asset.
D. A company is exempt from all legal obligations.
11. Which of the following is a characteristic of a limited liability company?
A. Its owners must pay every company debt from personal funds in all circumstances.
B. It cannot employ staff.
C. It has no legal obligations.
D. Its shareholders' liability is generally limited to the extent provided by law.
12. What is the main function of shareholders in a company?
A. To own shares and exercise the rights attached to those shares.
B. To perform every daily management task personally.
C. To audit all company transactions themselves.
D. To guarantee every company loan personally.
13. Who is primarily responsible for overseeing a company's strategic direction and governance?
A. Ordinary customers.
B. Delivery drivers.
C. The board of directors.
D. Competitors.
14. What is the principal purpose of a company's constitution or equivalent governing document?
A. To advertise the company's products.
B. To establish rules governing the company's internal management and operations.
C. To record employees' daily attendance only.
D. To replace all national laws.
15. What distinguishes a public limited company from a typical private company?
A. It is prohibited from making profits.
B. It cannot employ directors.
C. It must be owned by a single individual.
D. Subject to applicable law, it may offer shares to the public.
16. Which form of business is owned and operated by its members for their mutual benefit?
A. Cooperative society.
B. Sole proprietorship.
C. Private household.
D. Public department.
17. What is a franchise?
A. A business that is prohibited from using established brands.
B. A government-issued personal identity document.
C. An arrangement allowing a franchisee to operate using another party's brand and business system under agreed
terms.
D. A partnership that automatically dissolves after one month.
18. What is a multinational corporation?
A. A business operating in only one neighbourhood.
B. A business with operations in more than one country.
C. A company that sells products only to its employees.
D. An organisation that has no management structure.
19. Which of the following is an example of a public-sector organisation?
A. A privately owned clothing shop.
B. A family-owned restaurant.
C. A privately owned supermarket.
D. A government-owned public utility.
20. What is a non-profit organisation primarily established to do?
A. Pursue a mission or public benefit rather than distribute profits to owners.
B. Distribute all its income to shareholders.
C. Avoid all financial reporting requirements.
D. Operate exclusively for the personal benefit of its founder.
21. What is meant by business ownership?
A. The act of purchasing goods for personal consumption.
B. The process of supervising employees without authority.
C. The legal or recognised right to own and exercise control over a business.
D. The preparation of advertisements only.
22. Which of the following is an internal stakeholder in a business organisation?
A. A government regulator.
B. An employee.
C. A supplier.
D. A customer.
23. Which of the following is an external stakeholder?
A. A department manager.
B. An employee.
C. An internal auditor employed by the company.
D. A supplier.
24. What is the purpose of an organisational structure?
A. To define reporting relationships, responsibilities and lines of authority.
B. To prevent employees from communicating.
C. To eliminate the need for management.
D. To ensure that all employees perform identical tasks.
25. In a hierarchical organisational structure, authority generally flows:
A. From customers to suppliers only.
B. Without any defined reporting relationships.
C. From higher levels of management to lower levels.
D. From competitors to employees.
26. What is delegation in business management?
A. Transferring ownership of the entire business to customers.
B. Assigning authority and responsibility for specific tasks to another person.
C. Refusing to assign tasks to employees.
D. Closing a department permanently.
27. What is centralisation?
A. Giving every employee complete authority over the entire business.
B. Eliminating senior management positions.
C. Transferring all decisions to customers.
D. Concentrating decision-making authority at higher levels of the organisation.
28. What is decentralisation?
A. Distributing decision-making authority to lower levels or different units of an organisation.
B. Concentrating all decisions in one person's hands.
C. Removing all departmental responsibilities.
D. Eliminating communication between branches.
29. Which business function is primarily concerned with recruiting, training and developing employees?
A. Marketing.
B. Production.
C. Human resources management.
D. Procurement.
30. Which department is mainly responsible for preparing financial statements and maintaining accounting
records?
A. Public relations.
B. Finance and accounting.
C. Human resources.
D. Production.
31. What is the primary purpose of marketing in a business organisation?
A. To prevent the business from understanding customer needs.
B. To eliminate the need for product quality.
C. To prepare employee contracts only.
D. To identify customer needs and create, communicate and deliver value.
32. Which of the following is an example of an operational activity?
A. Manufacturing products or delivering services to customers.
B. Determining the national constitution.
C. Issuing passports on behalf of a foreign government.
D. Conducting a national election.
33. What is corporate social responsibility (CSR)?
A. The practice of avoiding all environmental obligations.
B. The process of maximising sales regardless of consequences.
C. A business's responsibility to consider its social, environmental and ethical impacts.
D. A rule requiring every company to operate as a charity.
34. What is business ethics?
A. The process of increasing prices without justification.
B. The principles and standards used to guide responsible business conduct.
C. A method of avoiding customer complaints by ignoring them.
D. The practice of concealing all financial information from regulators.
35. Why is compliance with laws and regulations important to business organisations?
A. It guarantees that competitors will close.
B. It removes the need for management.
C. It allows organisations to ignore contractual obligations.
D. It helps businesses meet legal requirements and reduce the risk of penalties.
36. What is a business merger?
A. The combination of two or more businesses into a single business entity or combined operation.
B. The dismissal of every employee in a company.
C. The closure of a business without transferring its activities.
D. The purchase of office stationery.
37. What is a business acquisition?
A. A business's decision to stop serving customers.
B. The recruitment of a new employee.
C. One business obtaining control of another business or its assets.
D. A government department preparing its annual budget.
38. Which factor is important when selecting an appropriate form of business organisation?
A. The colour of the office furniture alone.
B. Capital requirements, liability, ownership and legal obligations.
C. The owner's favourite television programme.
D. The number of advertisements produced by competitors only.
39. What is a business plan?
A. A list of customers who have complained.
B. A document containing only the business owner's personal details.
C. A record of daily employee attendance only.
D. A document outlining business objectives, strategies, operations and financial projections.
40. Which of the following best describes effective corporate governance?
A. The system through which an organisation is directed, controlled and held accountable.
B. Allowing directors to operate without oversight.
C. Preventing shareholders from exercising any lawful rights.
D. Removing all internal controls and reporting procedures.
