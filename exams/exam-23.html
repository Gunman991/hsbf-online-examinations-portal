Examination questions

Question 1

Auditing and investigation (2 marks) An organisation discovers unexplained shortages of inventory and suspects that goods may have been removed without authorisation. Which statement best distinguishes an investigation from a routine audit?

Question 2

Enquiry (2 marks) A warehouse supervisor claims that all goods recorded as dispatched were delivered to customers. The auditor wants to establish whether the claim is accurate. What is the most appropriate approach?

Question 3

Analytical procedures (2 marks) A transport company records the following fuel consumption for three comparable trucks: Truck Distance travelled Fuel consumed A 2,400 km 360 litres B 2,000 km 300 litres C 2,400 km 600 litres Which truck requires the greatest further investigation?

Question 4

Reperformance (2 marks) An accounts clerk prepares a bank reconciliation. The auditor independently repeats the reconciliation using the cash book and bank statement and identifies an omitted transaction. Which technique has been applied?

Question 5

Recalculation (2 marks) A supplier invoices a company for 240 units at $18 per unit. The invoice total is $4,500, and no additional charges are permitted under the purchase agreement. What is the overstatement?

Question 6

Inspection of documents (2 marks) A company orders 500 units at $12 each. The goods received note records 420 units, but the supplier invoice charges for all 500 units. What should the auditor do first?

Question 7

External confirmation (2 marks) The company's supplier ledger shows an outstanding balance of $27,800. The supplier independently confirms a balance of $24,600 at the same date. Which is the most appropriate audit response?

Question 8

Physical asset inspection (2 marks) An asset register lists 35 laptops. The auditor selects 10 entries from the register and physically locates only 8 laptops. What is the principal issue identified by this test?

Question 9

Observation (2 marks) Company policy requires a security guard to verify an authorised dispatch note before allowing a truck to leave the warehouse. During a site visit, the auditor observes a truck leaving after the driver receives only verbal permission. What is the strongest conclusion?

Question 10

Vouching and tracing (2 marks) An auditor selects a payment recorded in the cash book and follows it back to the supplier invoice, approved purchase order and goods received note. Which technique is being applied?

Question 11

Stock reconciliation (2 marks) The inventory records show 1,250 units, but the physical count identifies 1,180 units. No authorised adjustments have yet been identified. What is the initial stock discrepancy?

Question 12

Audit sampling (2 marks) An auditor selects 40 expense claims from a population of 800 claims, concentrating on unusually large claims and those submitted at month-end. Which statement best describes this approach?

Question 13

Internal control testing (2 marks) Company policy requires the procurement manager to approve all purchases above $2,000. The auditor examines 50 purchases above this limit and finds that 10 have no evidence of approval. What percentage of the sampled purchases lacks evidence of approval?

Question 14

Computer-assisted audit techniques (2 marks) An auditor uses a spreadsheet to identify supplier payments with identical invoice numbers, supplier names and amounts. What is the main purpose of this procedure?

Question 15

Audit evidence and reporting (2 marks) An auditor identifies that three out of 20 dispatches lacked the independent verification required by company policy. Which audit finding is most appropriate?

Question 16 — Written / Scenario Response

Integrated audit and loss investigation Case study: Mavambo Wholesale and Distribution Company Mavambo Wholesale and Distribution Company has experienced increasing operating costs, supplier payment disputes and unexplained inventory shortages. Management has appointed an internal auditor to investigate the company's purchasing, inventory, fuel and payment systems. During the initial review, the auditor discovers the following: Finding 1: Supplier invoice discrepancy The company received an invoice for 400 bags of cement at $15 per bag, totalling $6,000. However, the goods received note records only 340 bags. The accounts department has prepared the full $6,000 for payment. Finding 2: Unexplained inventory shortage The inventory records show 800 bags of fertiliser. A physical count identifies only 730 bags. The warehouse supervisor explains that some fertiliser was issued urgently to customers without dispatch notes. Finding 3: Unusual fuel consumption Two comparable delivery trucks travelled similar distances under similar operating conditions. Truck Distance travelled Fuel consumed Truck A 3,000 km 450 litres Truck B 3,000 km 600 litres The fleet manager explains that Truck B may have a mechanical problem. However, no recent maintenance report is available. Finding 4: Possible duplicate supplier payments A spreadsheet analysis identifies two payments of $2,400 each to the same supplier, carrying identical invoice references. The accounts clerk states that both payments were legitimate but has not provided supporting documents. Finding 5: Weak dispatch controls During a site visit, the auditor observes a security guard allowing a delivery truck to leave after receiving verbal permission from a warehouse supervisor. No authorised dispatch note is checked, despite company policy requiring documentary authorisation. Required a. Supplier invoice investigation (4 marks) i. Recalculate the value of the cement recorded as received. (2 marks) ii. Calculate the amount that should be withheld from payment pending investigation, assuming no additional delivery is evidenced. (2 marks) b. Inventory investigation (4 marks) i. Calculate the inventory shortage. (1 mark) ii. Explain three practical audit or investigative procedures the auditor should perform to establish the cause of the shortage. (3 marks) c. Fuel consumption analysis (4 marks) i. Calculate fuel consumption per 100 km for each truck. (2 marks) ii. Identify the unusual result and explain two possible causes that should be investigated before concluding that fuel theft has occurred. (2 marks) d. Duplicate payments and dispatch controls (4 marks) i. Explain two procedures the auditor should perform to investigate the suspected duplicate payments. (2 marks) ii. Recommend two practical improvements to strengthen the dispatch control system. (2 marks) e. Audit evidence and reporting (4 marks) Prepare a brief audit finding on the observed dispatch control weakness. Your answer must include:  The condition identified.  The relevant company policy or criterion.  One risk arising from the weakness.  One practical recommendation. Award 1 mark for each element.
