Question 21 — Written / Scenario Response

INTERNAL CONTROLS IN THE PURCHASING SYSTEM 30 Marks Scenario: ABC Manufacturing Company ABC Manufacturing Company is a medium-sized business that purchases raw materials, spare parts, stationery and equipment for its operations. The company has experienced an increase in procurement costs, unexplained inventory shortages and complaints from management about supplier payments. During a recent review, the risk and loss control officer discovered the following: 1. Departmental managers frequently request goods verbally. Purchase requisitions are not always prepared, and there is no central register of outstanding requests. 2. The purchasing officer selects suppliers without consistently obtaining competitive quotations. Some purchases are repeatedly awarded to the same supplier without documented justification. 3. The purchasing officer can create new supplier accounts in the accounting system without independent verification or approval. There is no formal process for checking whether suppliers are genuine. 4. The purchasing officer prepares purchase orders and sends them to suppliers without obtaining the required management approval. In some cases, purchase orders are prepared after the goods have already been delivered. 5. When goods arrive, warehouse staff sign delivery notes without counting or inspecting the goods. They rely on the supplier's delivery note and do not always compare the delivery with the purchase order. 6. Goods Received Notes are not consistently prepared. When they are prepared, they are not always independently checked against the quantities actually received. 7. The accounts payable clerk processes supplier invoices using the supplier's invoice alone. Purchase orders and Goods Received Notes are not routinely checked before invoices are entered into the accounting system. 8. The purchasing officer can amend supplier bank details, while the accounts clerk can prepare supplier payments and update accounting records. The same accounts clerk can also approve payments when the finance manager is unavailable, without a documented alternative approval process. 9. Supplier statements are rarely reconciled with the accounts payable ledger. Management only investigates discrepancies when a supplier complains about delayed or incorrect payments. 10. Excess raw materials and spare parts have accumulated in the warehouse because purchases are not consistently checked against inventory levels or approved budgets. Required (a) Identify and explain SIX internal control weaknesses in ABC Manufacturing Company's purchasing system. For each weakness, explain the risk or possible consequence to the organisation. (18 marks) (b) Recommend SIX appropriate mitigation measures that ABC Manufacturing Company should implement to strengthen its purchasing and procurement controls. Each recommendation must address a relevant weakness identified in part (a). (12 marks) Total: 30 Marks

Question 22 — Written / Scenario Response

INTERNAL CONTROLS IN THE SALES SYSTEM 30 Marks Scenario: Mavambo Wholesale Distributors Mavambo Wholesale Distributors sells groceries, beverages and household products to supermarkets, small retailers and individual customers. Most of its sales are made on credit, although some customers pay cash. Management has noticed declining cash collections, increasing customer complaints, unexplained inventory differences and a growing balance of overdue receivables. A risk and loss control review identified the following weaknesses: 1. Sales representatives accept customer orders by telephone or through informal messages. Some orders are not entered into the sales system, and there is no effective register for tracking all customer orders. 2. Sales representatives can approve new credit customers and increase existing customers' credit limits without independent authorisation. Credit checks are not consistently performed. 3. The warehouse releases goods based on verbal instructions from sales representatives. In some cases, goods are dispatched before an approved sales order is available. 4. Warehouse staff prepare delivery notes manually, but the documents are not always sequentially numbered. Customers' signatures are sometimes missing from delivery notes, and proof of delivery is not consistently returned to the accounts department. 5. Sales invoices are prepared after dispatch, but there is no independent check to ensure that every dispatch has resulted in an invoice. Some goods are delivered without being invoiced, and invoices sometimes contain incorrect prices or quantities. 6. Sales representatives can offer discounts outside the approved pricing policy. There is no documented authorisation process for exceptional discounts. 7. Cash received from customers is sometimes collected by sales representatives and kept in their offices before being handed to the cashier. Receipts are not always issued immediately, and cash collections are not independently checked against customer accounts. 8. The same employee can receive customer payments, update the receivables ledger and approve adjustments to customer balances. 9. Credit notes are sometimes issued when customers complain about damaged or missing goods, without independent verification of the complaint or evidence that goods were returned. 10. The receivables ledger is not regularly reconciled to the control account. Overdue customer balances are not systematically reviewed, and follow-up action depends on individual sales representatives. Required (a) Identify and explain SIX internal control weaknesses in Mavambo Wholesale Distributors' sales system. For each weakness, explain the associated risk or possible consequence to the organisation. (18 marks) (b) Recommend SIX appropriate mitigation measures that management should implement to strengthen controls over customer orders, credit approval, dispatch, invoicing, cash collection and receivables. Each recommendation must address a relevant weakness identified in part (a). (12 marks) Total: 30 Marks END OF EXAMINATION Reminder to students: Strong answers do more than list weaknesses. They explain why a weakness creates risk and recommend a practical, specific and effective control to reduce that risk.
