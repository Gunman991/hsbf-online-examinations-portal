Question 31 — Written / Scenario Response

Selecting and managing an international transport solution Zimbabwe Copper Components (ZCC) manufactures specialised copper components for industrial customers. It has received an export order from a customer in Germany for 8 tonnes of components with a total invoice value of US$120,000. The buyer requires delivery within 18 days. The components are valuable, must be protected against moisture and physical damage, and cannot be delivered late without exposing ZCC to contractual penalties. The logistics department has obtained the following indicative quotations: Item Option A: Air freight Option B: Sea freight Main international freight $18,000 $4,800 Origin and destination handling $1,500 $3,200 Estimated transit time 5–8 days 22–30 days The quotations exclude some other potential charges, and the company has not yet confirmed the full door-to-door cost of either option. The buyer has requested shipment tracking, proper export documentation and proof of delivery. Required: (a) Evaluate the two transport options and recommend the most appropriate solution, considering cost, transit time, reliability and the buyer's delivery deadline. (6 marks) (b) Calculate the quoted freight and handling cost for each option. Calculate the difference between the two quoted totals. Explain why these figures alone are insufficient to determine the full landed or delivered cost. (4 marks) (c) Explain five actions ZCC should take to plan, document, monitor and complete the export shipment successfully. (5 marks) (d) Explain how Incoterms® and a clearly agreed division of responsibilities can help ZCC and its buyer avoid misunderstandings about delivery, risk transfer, transport costs and insurance. Question 32 — Written / Scenario Response

Cross-border mineral exports and logistics risk management Great Zimbabwe Minerals exports copper concentrate from a mine in Zimbabwe to an overseas customer. The consignment must travel by road to a regional seaport and then by sea to the destination country. During planning, the logistics manager identifies the following circumstances:  The export contract does not clearly define the point at which risk transfers from seller to buyer.  The estimated weight on the packing list differs from the weight recorded during loading.  The company has not confirmed all export permits and customs requirements.  A road transporter has been selected mainly because it offered the lowest quotation, but its safety record has not been checked.  The cargo is valuable, and the route includes locations where vehicles may be vulnerable to theft.  The shipment is scheduled to arrive at the port shortly before a public holiday, when terminal operations may be restricted.  The company has not confirmed the scope of cargo insurance or the applicable exclusions.  Heavy rainfall and poor road conditions may delay the journey to the port.  The customer has not received a confirmed shipping schedule or regular status updates.  The finance department has not accounted for foreign exchange movements and possible additional port charges in its export-cost forecast. Required: Identify eight major risks from the scenario. For each risk: 1. Explain the potential operational, financial, legal or customer-service consequences. 2. Recommend a specific and practical mitigation measure. Select the eight most significant risks and avoid repeating the same risk under different names. (8 risks × 2.5 marks = 20 marks)
