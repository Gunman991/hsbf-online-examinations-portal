SECTION A
1. What is the primary function of the judiciary in Zimbabwe?
A. To create national budgets
B. To interpret and apply the law
C. To enforce tax collection
D. To formulate government policies
2. Which court is the highest court in Zimbabwe in constitutional matters?
A. High Court
B. Supreme Court
C. Constitutional Court
D. Magistrates Court
3. Which court generally handles ordinary civil disputes involving contractual claims within its jurisdiction?
A. Magistrates Court
B. Constitutional Court
C. Supreme Court only
D. Traditional court only
4. What is the main function of the Supreme Court of Zimbabwe?
A. To register all commercial businesses
B. To hear appeals within its jurisdiction
C. To draft contracts for companies
D. To investigate every criminal offence
5. Which court generally has unlimited original jurisdiction in civil and criminal matters, subject to constitutional and statutory provisions?
A. Small Claims Court
B. Magistrates Court
C. High Court
D. Traditional court
6. A party disagrees with a judgment delivered by a lower court and believes a legal error occurred. What may the party seek, where permitted by law?
A. An appeal
B. A new contract
C. Automatic compensation
D. A business licence
7. Which of the following is a civil dispute?
A. A prosecution for armed robbery
B. A dispute over payment under a commercial contract
C. A prosecution for murder
D. A criminal prosecution for assault
8. What is the primary purpose of the Small Claims Court?
A. To determine constitutional disputes
B. To hear eligible minor civil claims under applicable limits
C. To hear every criminal appeal
D. To supervise Parliament
9. Which principle requires a court to apply the law fairly and impartially?
A. Judicial independence
B. Commercial secrecy
C. Parliamentary privilege
D. Contractual freedom
10. A company sues a supplier for failing to deliver goods as agreed. The case is primarily a:
A. Constitutional dispute
B. Civil dispute
C. Criminal prosecution
D. Parliamentary proceeding
11. Which institution is responsible for administering justice through the courts in Zimbabwe?
A. The judiciary
B. The police service alone
C. The tax authority
D. The business registration office
12. What does original jurisdiction mean?
A. The authority to hear a case for the first time
B. The authority to amend the Constitution
C. The authority to enforce every foreign judgment automatically
D. The authority to hear appeals only
13. Why is it important to identify the correct court before commencing legal proceedings?
A. Every court has identical powers
B. It determines the applicable court jurisdiction and procedure
C. It guarantees that the claimant will win
D. It removes the need to present evidence
14. What is a contract?
A. Any conversation between two people
B. An agreement that creates legally enforceable obligations
C. A promise that can never be enforced
D. A document used only by large companies
15. Which of the following is generally an essential requirement for a valid contract under Zimbabwean law?
A. A witness must always be present
B. The agreement must always be registered
C. The parties must have the required contractual capacity
D. The agreement must always be written in English
16. What is an offer in contract law?
A. A definite proposal made with the intention of creating a contract upon acceptance
B. A court order requiring payment
C. A refusal to enter into an agreement
D. A promise made only after a dispute arises
17. When does acceptance generally occur?
A. When the offeree agrees to the offer in the legally required manner
B. Whenever the offeror changes their mind
C. When a third party hears about the offer
D. Only when a court approves the agreement
18. What is consideration in common-law contract principles?
A. The personal opinion of the judge
B. Something of value exchanged as part of a bargain
C. The physical appearance of a contract
D. The punishment for breach of contract
19. Which statement best describes contractual capacity?
A. The ability to advertise products
B. The legal ability to enter into a binding contract
C. The ability to borrow money from any lender
D. The ability to represent oneself in every court
20. A shop displays a product with a price tag. In many ordinary retail situations, the display is generally treated as:
A. A court judgment
B. An invitation to treat
C. A completed sale in every circumstance
D. A breach of contract
21. Which of the following may make a contract voidable, depending on the applicable law and circumstances?
A. Certain forms of misrepresentation
B. Proper performance of all obligations
C. Correct delivery of goods
D. Payment made as agreed
22. What is the purpose of contractual terms?
A. To identify the rights and obligations of the parties
B. To replace all laws governing business
C. To guarantee that no dispute can occur
D. To eliminate the need for agreement
23. Two businesses agree that one will supply 100 computers and the other will pay the agreed price. This agreement is primarily an example of:
A. A constitutional amendment
B. A commercial contract
C. A criminal sentence
D. A judicial precedent
24. What is the general effect of a lawful and valid contract?
A. It creates enforceable obligations between the parties
B. It automatically binds every person in the country
C. It allows either party to ignore agreed terms
D. It removes the jurisdiction of all courts
25. Which situation most clearly demonstrates consent obtained through duress?
A. A buyer voluntarily accepts a fair price
B. A seller delivers goods on time
C. A person agrees to a contract because of unlawful threats
D. A business pays an invoice voluntarily
26. Why should parties clearly identify the subject matter of a commercial contract?
A. To make the agreement more difficult to understand
B. To reduce uncertainty about what must be supplied or performed
C. To prevent courts from interpreting the agreement
D. To eliminate all contractual obligations
27. A supplier and buyer agree on the goods, price and delivery date. Which action best helps establish their agreed terms?
A. Relying exclusively on rumours
B. Keeping clear records of the agreement
C. Ignoring written communications
D. Allowing an unrelated person to change the terms
28. What is a breach of contract?
A. The lawful performance of an agreement
B. The failure to perform a contractual obligation as required
C. The creation of a new business
D. The registration of a company
29. A supplier agrees to deliver goods on 10 October but fails to deliver them without lawful justification. This may constitute:
A. Breach of contract
B. Automatic contract renewal
C. Judicial independence
D. A valid acceptance
30. Which of the following is a possible remedy for breach of contract?
A. Contractual damages
B. Automatic imprisonment in every case
C. Cancellation of the Constitution
D. Automatic transfer of company ownership
31. What is the primary purpose of contractual damages?
A. To punish every party who loses a dispute
B. To compensate for legally recognised loss caused by the breach
C. To guarantee that the claimant makes a profit
D. To prevent all future business transactions
32. A buyer refuses to pay for goods properly delivered under a valid contract. What may the seller have a right to do?
A. Seek an appropriate legal remedy for non-payment
B. Automatically imprison the buyer
C. Seize any property belonging to the buyer without legal authority
D. Rewrite the contract unilaterally in every circumstance
33. What does specific performance generally mean?
A. An order requiring a party to perform a contractual obligation
B. An automatic award of criminal punishment
C. A refusal to recognise any agreement
D. A transfer of a case to Parliament
34. When may cancellation be available as a remedy for breach of contract?
A. Whenever a party dislikes the agreed price
B. When the applicable legal requirements for cancellation are satisfied
C. Whenever a contract becomes inconvenient
D. Whenever one party finds a better business opportunity
35. A business suffers financial loss because a supplier delivers defective goods. In seeking damages, the business will generally need to establish:
A. Only that it owns a business
B. The relevant breach, legally recoverable loss and required causal connection
C. That the supplier is unpopular
D. That every contract must be registered
36. Which situation is an example of anticipatory breach?
A. A party announces before performance is due that it will not fulfil its contractual obligations
B. A party performs every obligation on time
C. A buyer accepts goods without complaint
D. A court interprets a statutory provision
37. What is the purpose of a force majeure clause in a contract?
A. To set the court's annual budget
B. To address specified extraordinary events that may affect contractual performance
C. To guarantee profits for both parties
D. To make every breach a criminal offence
38. A business claims damages for a breach but deliberately ignores reasonable steps that could have reduced its loss. Which principle may become relevant?
A. Mitigation of loss
B. Judicial appointment
C. Parliamentary sovereignty alone
D. Contract registration
39. A party fails to perform because the other party prevented the agreed performance. What is the most appropriate legal approach?
A. Automatically hold the first party liable in every circumstance
B. Examine the contract, the facts and the legal consequences of the other party's conduct
C. Assume that no contract ever existed
D. Automatically award the maximum possible damages
40. Two businesses disagree over a breach of contract. Which is generally the most appropriate first step, where circumstances permit?
A. Destroy the relevant records
B. Review the contract and attempt to resolve the dispute through appropriate communication or dispute-resolution procedures
C. Publicly accuse the other party without evidence
D. Ignore the dispute indefinitely

Question 1
A contract is a legally binding agreement which can be written or oral. Intention is fundamental in a binding contract. Four factors must be present in order for a contract to be binding which are offer, acceptance, agreement and consideration. If a party breaches a contract the other party may sue for damages.
Explain briefly giving examples where necessary the following contract terms;
Offer (5)
Acceptance (5)
Agreement (5)
Consideration (5)
Total mark (20 marks)
Question 2
Lise, a wholesaler from Scotland entered into a contract with Pascal, a manufacturer from Poland to supply her with machinery that was to be built according to her specific design.
Several months into Pascal's build process, Lise decided that she did not require the machines after all notified Pascal of her intention not to pay for the machines.
Required
(a) State the type of breach of contract committed that Lise committed and state when it occurs. (5 marks)
(b) Explain the most appropriate remedy for Pascal for Lise's breach of contract. (5 marks)
(c) Describe repudiatory breach (5 marks)
(e) Explain anticipatory breach (5 marks)
Total 20 marks

(20 marks)
Question 4

Differentiate between civil and criminal laws (10 marks)
Total 20 marks
