Examination questions

Question 1

Segregation of duties (2 marks) A purchasing officer at a wholesale business raises purchase orders, approves supplier invoices and authorises payments. Which internal control weakness is most evident?

Question 2

Purchase requisitions (2 marks) A department requests 200 boxes of stationery without providing evidence of need or approval. What is the most appropriate control?

Question 3

Supplier selection (2 marks) A purchasing manager repeatedly awards contracts to a relative's company without obtaining quotations from other suppliers. Which control would best address this risk?

Question 4

Goods received (2 marks) A business orders 1,000 units of a product but receives only 900. The supplier invoices the business for all 1,000 units. What should the accounts payable department do?

Question 5

Three-way matching (2 marks) What is the primary purpose of matching a purchase order, goods received note and supplier invoice?

Question 6

Supplier invoice verification (2 marks) An invoice contains a unit price of $12, although the approved purchase order specifies $10. The quantity received is correct. What is the most appropriate action?

Question 7

Credit sales (2 marks) A salesperson approves credit sales to customers without checking their credit limits. Several customers subsequently fail to pay. Which control would most directly reduce this risk?

Question 8

Dispatch of goods (2 marks) Warehouse staff release goods based on verbal instructions from sales representatives, without approved customer orders or dispatch documents. What is the main risk?

Question 9

Sales invoicing (2 marks) A company dispatches goods but sometimes fails to prepare sales invoices. Which control would best address the problem?

Question 10

Cash receipts (2 marks) A cashier receives customer payments, updates customer accounts and prepares the bank reconciliation. Which control improvement is most appropriate?

Question 11

Bank reconciliation (2 marks) The cash receipts records show $8,500, but the corresponding bank deposit is $7,750. What should management do first?

Question 12

Supplier bank details (2 marks) An employee receives an email requesting a supplier's bank account details to be changed immediately. What is the strongest control?

Question 13

Credit notes and sales returns (2 marks) A sales clerk can issue credit notes without supporting documentation or supervisory approval. What is the most significant risk?

Question 14

Detective controls (2 marks) Which of the following is the clearest example of a detective control in a sales system?

Question 15

Independent review (2 marks) A manager notices that monthly sales have increased by 45%, while bank deposits have remained almost unchanged. What is the most appropriate response?

Question 16 — Written / Scenario Response

Investigation of internal control weaknesses in purchasing and sales systems Case study: Mavambo Wholesale (Pvt) Ltd Mavambo Wholesale is a growing distribution business that sells groceries and household products to retailers on both cash and credit terms. The company has recently experienced cash shortages, unexplained inventory losses, increasing supplier balances and customer complaints about incorrect invoices. The managing director has asked you, as an internal control officer, to investigate the purchasing and sales systems and recommend improvements. During your investigation, you discover the following: Purchasing system 1. Departmental managers request goods verbally, and purchasing officers sometimes place orders without approved purchase requisitions. 2. The purchasing manager regularly buys from three preferred suppliers without obtaining competitive quotations. One supplier is owned by a close associate of the manager. 3. Goods received are recorded by warehouse employees, who sometimes sign delivery documents without physically counting or inspecting the goods. 4. The accounts department receives supplier invoices but does not consistently compare them with purchase orders and goods received notes before payment. 5. The same purchasing officer can create suppliers on the accounting system, amend supplier bank details and prepare payment instructions. 6. Supplier statements are not regularly reconciled with the company's accounts payable ledger. Sales system 7. Sales representatives accept customer orders and approve credit sales without checking customer credit limits. 8. Warehouse employees dispatch goods using verbal instructions, and some dispatches are not supported by sequentially numbered dispatch notes. 9. Sales invoices are prepared manually, and there is no independent check to ensure that every dispatch has been invoiced. 10. Cashiers receive customer payments, update customer accounts and prepare bank reconciliations. 11. Credit notes for returned goods are issued by sales clerks without independent approval or evidence that the goods were returned. 12. The company's monthly sales reports show increasing revenue, but bank deposits have not increased correspondingly. Management has not investigated the differences. Required (a) Purchasing system — 8 marks Identify four internal control weaknesses in the purchasing system. For each weakness:  Explain the risk or possible consequence.  Recommend a practical internal control that management should introduce. (2 marks per weakness: 1 mark for the risk and 1 mark for the recommendation.) (b) Sales system — 8 marks Identify four internal control weaknesses in the sales system. For each weakness:  Explain the risk or possible consequence.  Recommend a practical internal control that management should introduce. (2 marks per weakness: 1 mark for the risk and 1 mark for the recommendation.) (c) Investigation and monitoring — 4 marks The managing director suspects that the company may be losing money through fraudulent purchases, unrecorded sales or cash theft. Explain two practical investigation procedures that you would perform to establish whether losses have occurred. For each procedure, explain the evidence you would examine and how the procedure would help identify irregularities. (2 marks per procedure.) End of Examination Total: 50 marks
